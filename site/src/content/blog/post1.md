Most people use AI to write faster. I use it to think better.

The framing shift happened when I stopped treating Claude Code as an assistant ("do this task") and started treating it as an advisory council ("help me think through this decision"). The difference is subtle but it changes everything about how you interact.

## Why I needed this

I'm the only PM fully assigned to my project. There's no second product person to gut-check a strategy doc, push back on a recommendation, or ask "have you thought about this from the stakeholder's perspective?" before it goes to leadership.

That's the gap the advisory council fills. It's how I red-team my own outputs before they leave my hands.

## What an advisory council does

When I'm facing a strategic decision or preparing a deliverable, I don't ask Claude for the answer. I ask it to play roles:

- **The skeptic:** "What's the strongest argument against this approach?"
- **The researcher:** "What does the evidence actually say about this assumption?"
- **The strategist:** "If both data sources are valid, what frame makes them compatible?"
- **The stakeholder sim:** "If you were [role], what would concern you about this recommendation?"

This isn't prompt engineering theater. It's a thinking methodology that produces better decisions because it forces me to consider perspectives I'd otherwise skip, especially when I'm the only one looking at the work before it ships.

## Where it actually helps

Before a strategy doc or recommendation goes to leadership, I run it through the council. The skeptic finds the weak spots. The stakeholder sim tells me what questions I'll get. The researcher checks whether my claims hold up under scrutiny.

It doesn't change the recommendation most of the time. But it changes how I present it, which arguments I lead with, which objections I preempt, and where I need stronger evidence. The deliverable that reaches stakeholders is sharper because it's already survived a challenge.

## What it doesn't replace

- Reading the room in a stakeholder meeting
- Building trust through consistent follow-through
- Making a call when the data is ambiguous and you just have to decide
- The emotional intelligence of knowing when to push and when to wait

AI sharpens thinking. It doesn't replace judgment. But when you're a solo PM, having a structured way to stress-test your own work before it reaches stakeholders is the difference between confidence and hope.
